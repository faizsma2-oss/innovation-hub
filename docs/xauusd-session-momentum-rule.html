XAUUSD Session Momentum Rule

Full specification of the rule as tested, with its results, limits and known problems. Prepared 26 October 2026. Council run real-20261026-095550.

What this document is. It describes one rule, called C4 in the council run, in enough detail that anyone can reproduce its signals, results and limits. Every figure comes from the code in the repository or from the saved runs named here.

What it is not. It is not a trading signal, a recommendation or financial advice. The rule has been tested on historical data only. A forward test on data nobody has looked at has not started. Read section 14 before drawing any conclusion.

2. Market and data

Prices come from Dukascopy's public feed of XAUUSD one-minute bid candles, stored in UTC. The downloader reads one file per day. It keeps each candle's open, high, low, close and volume, and drops minutes with zero volume. Those minutes are treated as having no trading. The loader also checks that prices fall inside gold's real range (above $900 from 2010 onward, below $10,000) so that a wrongly scaled file cannot pass.

The one-minute candles are combined into five-minute bars labelled by their opening time. A five-minute bar is only as complete as the one-minute candles inside it, so a bar can be missing or partial if a minute was dropped.

A session-day is kept only if at least 80% of its five-minute bars exist. The first weeks of data are used only to warm up the ATR and the EMA. The first trade in the training list is on 3 February 2020.

3. Sessions and time zones

Each session runs from 08:00 to 17:00 in its own local time zone. The zone is handled by the time-zone database, so daylight saving moves the session in UTC automatically. For example, in March 2024 New York opens at 12:00 UTC and London at 08:00 UTC, while in other weeks New York opens at 13:00 UTC. Weekends are excluded: a session needs a Monday to Friday local date.

Each session is a grid of 108 five-minute bars (9 hours). The bars are indexed from 0 at 08:00 to 107 at 16:55. The decision bar is index 5, the bar that ends at 08:30.

4. Daily ATR

The ATR measures the typical range of a trading day, and it sets every distance in the rule. It is calculated as follows.

  1. A trading day runs from 17:00 New York time to 17:00 New York time the next day. Each one-minute candle is assigned to the day that its time falls in.
  2. For each trading day, take the high, low and close.
  3. True range for a day is the largest of: high minus low; high minus the previous day's close (its absolute value); low minus the previous day's close (its absolute value).
  4. The ATR for a day is the average of the true range over the 14 trading days before it. It uses earlier days only.
ATR(today) = mean of TrueRange over the 14 trading days before today

The rule uses ATR as it stood before the session began. Nothing from the decision day itself is used.

5. The 08:30 decision

5.1 Pre-session start price

The pre-session start price is the open of the first five-minute bar in the window below.

  • London: from 00:00 to 08:00 London time on the same date. This is the Asian session.
  • New York: from 08:00 London time to 08:00 New York time. This is the London morning, on the same calendar date.

5.2 Move

Move = close of the 08:30 bar − pre-session start price

The move is compared with the ATR as a multiple: Move in ATR = Move ÷ ATR.

5.3 Trend filter

A 300-bar exponential moving average (EMA) of five-minute closes is calculated across the whole series, with smoothing factor 2 ÷ (300 + 1). The filter uses the EMA value at the 08:30 bar.

5.4 Entry conditions

SideAll of these must hold at the 08:30 bar
BuyMove ≥ +0.6 × ATR, and the 08:30 close is above the 300-bar EMA.
SellMove ≤ −0.6 × ATR, and the 08:30 close is below the 300-bar EMA.
NeitherNo trade in that session.

The entry price is the 08:30 close. No slippage is added to the entry, but the spread is charged on the trade (section 7).

6. Stop, target and exit

ItemDefinition
Stop distance0.6 × ATR (the ATR from section 4).
Stop priceEntry minus the stop distance for a buy; entry plus it for a sell.
Target distance2 × the stop distance.
Target priceEntry plus the target distance for a buy; entry minus it for a sell.
Checked barsOnly the bars after the entry bar (from 08:35 onward) can hit the stop or the target.
Stop hitThe bar's low is at or below the stop for a buy, or its high is at or above the stop for a sell.
Target hitThe bar's high is at or above the target for a buy, or its low is at or below the target for a sell.
Both hit in one barCounted as a stop.
Gap through the stopIf the bar opens beyond the stop, the stop fills at that bar's open, which is worse than the stop price.
Gap through the targetIf the bar opens beyond the target, the target fills at that bar's open, which is better than the target price.
Session closeIf neither is hit, the trade closes at the close of the last bar of the session (the 16:55 bar), which is the 17:00 close.
Missing barsA missing bar cannot trigger a stop or target. See section 13.

Slippage of $0.10 per ounce is charged on stop exits, always against the trader. It is not charged on target or session-close exits.

7. Costs and the R result

R = ( direction × (exit price − entry price) − spread ) ÷ stop distance
direction = +1 for a buy, −1 for a sell
spread = $0.30 per ounce, once per trade
exit price for a stop = stop fill − direction × $0.10

A full stop with no gap gives about −1.03R, because of the spread and slippage. A target gives about +1.98R. These are the figures in the trade lists.

8. Filters that are switched off

The rule supports two more filters. Both are switched off in C4:

  • Volatility filter: off (threshold 0.0). When on, it would skip days whose ATR is below a set multiple of its 50-day median.
  • Trend filter: on, as described in section 5.3.

The following are not part of the rule: trade volume, the signal score from the step 2 scoring model, news, weekdays, time of year, and any manual judgement.

9. Simulation settings

These are settings for the simulation, not part of the market rule. They were confirmed by the user on 26 October 2026.

The broker's smallest XAUUSD size is not checked. At these limits, 29 of the 68 holdout trades would be below 1 oz, and many brokers set a minimum of 1 oz (0.01 lot). Check the minimum before using these sizes with any broker.

Uncertainty

With 68 holdout trades, the approximate 95% range for the average R is +0.05R to +0.51R, and for the win rate it is about 52% to 75%. A 50% win rate, which would be chance, falls just outside the holdout range and inside the training range. Treat the sample as small.

13. Known problems

  1. Optimistic fills. Entries are assumed at the exact 08:30 close. A real order would fill slightly later and often at a worse price.
  2. Missing bars can hide stop-outs. If a five-minute bar is missing, the backtester cannot see a stop inside it. A test confirmed this: a stop at −1R was recorded as a close at −0.25R. The holdout is not affected, because none of its 68 trades fell on a session-day with missing bars. The training list was not checked this way. This is not fixed yet.
  3. Dropped zero-volume minutes. The downloader removes one-minute candles with zero volume, so a quiet minute can leave a gap in the bars.
  4. Selection. 1,819 rule variants were tried on the training data before C4 was chosen. The holdout reduces that risk but does not remove it.
  5. The holdout is no longer untouched. It was used for the council verdict, the signal score, four session-close times and three spreads.
  6. Small sample. About 34 trades a year. A sample big enough to judge takes roughly two years.
  7. Bid prices. Dukascopy's bid prices are not a broker's prices. Real spreads widen around news and at the session opens, which is when the rule trades.
  8. Slippage. $0.10 per ounce is assumed on stops. Real slippage around news can be larger.
  9. The daily loss cap cannot bind alone. A full stop costs about $51, so two stops in one day exceed the $100 cap by about $3.
  10. Overlap. Two trades can be open at once in the hours when both sessions overlap.

15. What the rule does not do

  • It does not forecast prices. It applies fixed conditions to the 08:30 price on each day.
  • It does not use volume, news, the signal score, weekdays or seasons.
  • It does not trail stops, take partial profits or add to positions.
  • The simulation limits in section 9 are settings, not part of the rule.
  • It does not produce live signals. The journal records what the rule would have done, after each session closes.

17. Reproducing the results

From the repository root, on a Mac with the project's virtual environment:

python -m xauusd_council fetch --start 2020-01-01 --out data/xauusd_m1.csv
bash scripts/run_xauusd_council.sh
python -m xauusd_council journal --rule data/council/real-20261026-095550/result.json

The council run writes a transcript and a JSON record, including every trade's entry, stop, target, exit and result. The rule is stored in that record and can be loaded with Rule.from_dict. Other documents in docs/ hold the step-by-step record and the checklist.
